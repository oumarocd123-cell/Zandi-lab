About the lab

Advancing virology research at Morehouse School of Medicine.

Led by Dr. Keivan Zandi, our group investigates viral pathogens of global health importance and works to develop new antiviral strategies through mechanism-driven research.

Our focus

Research overview

The Zandi Virology Lab is based in the Department of [Department Name] at Morehouse School of Medicine. Our research centers on understanding the molecular and cellular mechanisms that underlie viral infection, with an emphasis on pathogens that disproportionately affect under-served communities and emerging global health threats.

We combine virological, biochemical, and cell biology approaches to identify viral vulnerabilities that can be targeted therapeutically. Ongoing work spans basic discovery — characterizing host-virus interactions — and applied efforts to evaluate small-molecule antivirals in preclinical models.

The lab welcomes motivated students and postdoctoral trainees committed to rigorous, collaborative science. Training in our group emphasizes experimental design, quantitative reasoning, and scientific communication.

Projects

Current directions

A selection of active research questions the lab is pursuing.

01 / Project

Antiviral Discovery

Screening and characterizing small-molecule inhibitors against clinically relevant viral targets, with a focus on mechanism of action and resistance profiling.

02 / Project

Host–Virus Interactions

Mapping the cellular pathways that viruses hijack for replication, and identifying host factors that can be targeted without compromising normal cell function.

03 / Project

Emerging Pathogens

Developing the reagents, models, and assays needed to respond to newly emerging viral threats relevant to public health in the Southeast and globally.

04 / Project

Translational Collaborations

Working with clinical and academic partners to move promising antiviral candidates toward preclinical validation and, where appropriate, toward early-stage clinical studies.

Our approach

How we work.

We believe the best science happens when rigorous experimental work is paired with a supportive, collaborative environment. Trainees in the lab take ownership of their projects from an early stage and are encouraged to pursue the questions that genuinely excite them.

We commit to reproducibility, to publishing our results regardless of whether they support our hypotheses, and to training the next generation of biomedical scientists — particularly those from backgrounds historically under-represented in research.
